A website project goes faster, costs less and ends better when you arrive prepared. Use this checklist before you speak to any developer or agency, including us.

## 1. Write down what the website must do

"We need a website" is not a goal. A goal sounds like "customers can book a consultation online" or "parents can find and register for our programme". Pick one main action and up to two supporting ones. Every page should help a visitor take one of them.

## 2. Know who the website is for

Describe your main visitor in a few lines: who they are, what they search for, and what they worry about before they buy. This shapes the words on your pages and the keywords you want to rank for.

## 3. Collect your content early

Delays almost always come from content, not code. Gather:

- Your logo in the best quality you have
- Photos of your team, work, classes or products
- The text for your main pages: home, about, services and contact
- Real testimonials you have permission to publish
- Your address, phone numbers, email and opening hours, written once and used everywhere

## 4. Collect examples you like

Save three to five websites you admire and note what you like about each: the layout, the colours, how simple it is to find information. Examples are the fastest way to explain taste.

## 5. Decide who approves work

Name one person who gives final feedback. Projects stall when five people send five different opinions.

## 6. Set a budget range and a deadline

Be honest about both. A good developer can tell you what is possible within your budget and suggest what to build first and what can wait for a later phase.

## 7. Ask about search visibility from the start

Ask how the site will handle page titles and descriptions, mobile speed, a sitemap and Google Business Profile. Adding these after launch costs more than building them in.
